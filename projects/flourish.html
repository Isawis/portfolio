Project: Flourish

Summary

Flourish is a mental health focused habit tracker based on growing digital plants as positive reinforcement. The project was a ambitious university project executed in 2021. The habit tracker supports a high level of personalisation as to provide a broad range of support depending on the user's need, although it's main purpose is to combat social isolation and encourage self care and keeping in touch with loved ones without inflicting overwhelm or feelings of pressure while doing so. The concept also includes a dynamic day-to-day customisation depending on whether the user is having a good or bad day, which affects the ambition and energy demand levels of the tasks. At the end of the project, we had a finished concept for a mental health tool we are proud to present as Flourish.

Goal

Flourish was designed with the intended purpose to help young adults living alone with declining mental health, through the Covid-19 pandemic. The focus is to help with daily tasks and to guide people back into healthy habits.

Background

Flourish is the result of a more ambitious university project executed during the COVID-19 pandemic 2021. The concept revolves around positive reinforcement for healthy habits, both physically and mentally, as well as breaking the trend of social isolation that plagued many young, single-person households around Sweden. This subject was chosen by the design team, as it was a very wide spread issue emerging from the sudden of environment. People all of a sudden went from meeting, talking to and having lunch with friends and colleagues daily, to working or watching lectures at home through their computers, sometimes not leaving the apartment at all for several days.

The concept is built around a reward system; a plant that grows as you complete tasks. This way, the plant grows together with the user. In order to not discourage the user, the plant does not wither when there's no activity in the app. The user is further encouraged through earning badges for continuous task completion. The experience is private by default, although the concept is also intended to promote and normalise transparency of mental health issues and encourages the user to embrace this journey alongside friends and family.

Design process

Background research

To kickstart the project, our first task was to explore the negative effects humans experienced as a result of the pandemic, hence finding the problem we wanted to address. Initial research included finding and analysing articles concerning health related consequences due to the pandemic. A pattern of social isolation was discovered, especially among young adults living alone. This initial research gave us a base for our design journey; a problem to solve, and a user group to interview.

User interviews

User interviews helped us dig deeper than the public articles and identify the key pain points in our user's mental health struggles. This further aided us in narrowing down the scope and clarify the goal of the project as well as key pain points for our user group.

Iteration

Mental health is a sensitive topic, and it's important to find the correct balance between motivation and overwhelm, as well as privacy and connection to others. Some feel that sharing their struggles with others helps them feel less alone, while others may still feel ashamed to the point where privacy must be waterproof. Thus, we designed the app to be able to fit everyone's needs, but still provide an out of the box and easy setup procedure, as to lower thresholds and not cause overwhelm.

A truly user centric process with plenty of iteration and user feedback loops was crucial to o strike this delicate balances. As we were 5 designers on the team, we also had a significant advantage in rapidly exploring multiple perspectives and design alternatives, hence exploring the design space thoroughly before converging our ideas at the end of each design phase.

Usability testing

As mentioned, this project this project included many iteration loops, and users were part of every step. Our main method of converging our design solution was by running our different versions through usability testing, and also following up on points of friction by a shorter interview at the end of the test. This allowed us to reach a consensus within the design team, truly shaped after user needs rather than personal biases within the design team. User tests were performed both during the wireframe phase and the latter stages with hi-fi prototypes.
